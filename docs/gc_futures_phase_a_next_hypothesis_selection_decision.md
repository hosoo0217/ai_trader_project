## 10. Selected prospective hypothesis

The selected hypothesis is:

> For two adjacent, complete, same-contract GC session segments separated only
> by an authoritative expected session or maintenance boundary, it may be
> possible to carry a complete immutable dependency-state reference into a
> shadow analysis of the next segment without changing detector semantics,
> backdating first-known evidence, or violating prefix invariance.

This is a feasibility hypothesis, not a claim that continuity is valid, that a
candidate exists, or that performance will improve.

## 11. Exact shadow-only boundary

Any future proposal may consider only one adjacent segment pair at a time. Both
segments must be:

- from the same canonical GC contract;
- individually complete and valid;
- ordered by strictly increasing normalized UTC moments;
- separated by an authoritative calendar/session boundary whose expected gap
  exactly reconciles to the observed gap; and
- free of roll, expiry, weekend, holiday closure, unscheduled outage, missing
  bar, duplicate bar, partial-session, or unresolved-calendar evidence.

Every other boundary is ineligible and must fail closed without carried state.

## 12. Complete dependency-closure requirement

Pool-only carry is forbidden. A future feasibility proposal must define and
validate the complete dependency closure needed by the unchanged V1 sequence:

- Equal Liquidity pool and lifecycle history;
- Dealing Range and lifecycle history;
- Liquidity Map classification and snapshot history;
- all source observations and first-known moments needed to validate them; and
- any later Structure Event and Fair Value Gap evidence formed in the receiving
  segment.

If complete foreign identities and causal histories cannot be validated from
the accepted inputs, the feasibility result is `STOP`, not partial continuity.

## 13. Immutable reference-only semantics

Carried state, if later proposed, must remain immutable reference evidence.
The receiving segment may not:

- recompute, mutate, enrich, repair, or renumber the source state;
- invent a transition at the boundary;
- reset first-known provenance;
- convert terminal state back to active state;
- replace a source identity with a hash of concatenated data; or
- use later receiving-segment observations to validate earlier source state.

Any new boundary or continuity identity must be versioned separately and must
reference, not overwrite, canonical source identities.

## 14. Chronology and no-look-ahead contract

The shadow feasibility analysis must process complete effective groups in
causal order. At each receiving-segment moment it may use only state and
observations known at or before that moment.

Future confirmation, FVG formation, target outcome, label horizon, return,
MFE/MAE, entry, exit, PnL, OOS membership, chart annotation, or human/LLM
judgment may not decide whether state is carried or eligible.

No evidence may be backdated to the start of the receiving segment.

## 18. Status and atomic processing

The status precedence remains:

`INVALID > AMBIGUOUS > UNKNOWN > VALID > NONE`

One boundary plus its complete carried dependency closure is an atomic group.
Missing, forked, contradictory, or incomplete state promotes nothing from that
group. Strictly prior complete shadow evidence may be preserved only when the
failing effective moment is determinable.

There is no silent sort, repair, gap fill, partial carry, or fallback to a
single detector.

## 19. Prefix invariance

historical insertion, calendar repair, tuple reorder, source replacement,
version mutation, or segment-boundary reclassification is ineligible for a
prefix comparison.

Both canonical and shadow arms must be byte-for-byte repeatable for identical
inputs.
